# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme manages cross-functional projects through a consistent lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Initiation confirms the business need with a project one-pager that defines the problem, measurable outcomes, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Work moves into planning when success metrics are clear, stakeholders agree on priorities, and team availability is confirmed.

Planning converts the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and a Definition of Done. During execution, teams use a GitHub Projects board to move work from Backlog through Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. Delivery follows small-pull-request conventions, regular sprint or milestone reviews, and weekly updates to the risk register and cross-team dependencies.

Clear role ownership keeps delivery aligned: the Project Manager coordinates plans, schedules, risks, and communications; the Product Manager sets outcomes and priorities; Developers implement and maintain tested software; and QA validates quality and acceptance criteria. Stakeholders provide business context and approvals, while the team works toward shared goals with customer-first, iterative, data-informed, and psychologically safe practices.

Communication combines 15-minute daily standups, weekly PM–PdM alignment, twice-weekly or agreed delivery-team standups, monthly stakeholder updates, demos, and structured escalation. Quality is maintained through unit and integration testing, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI linting and security scans, code review, and manual acceptance testing where needed. Releases require passing checks, drafted release notes, rollback or mitigation plans, staged verification, and stakeholder announcements; retrospectives capture owned, time-bound actions for continuous improvement.
